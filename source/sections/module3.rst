**Module 3: Surface Time Thieves**
==================================

.. _purpose-4:

**Purpose**
-----------

Help identify the patterns that steal time, disrupt flow, and quietly
drain capacity using RCD‑specific exercises inspired by the principles
in *Making Work Visible*.

**Time Thieves**
----------------

Time Thieves are the recurring patterns that quietly steal time, disrupt
flow, and make work feel harder than it should. Time Thieves show up
differently in RCD environments: interrupt‑driven work, unclear
handoffs, system dependencies, customer delays, and competing
priorities.

**Common Time Thieves**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Too Much WIP**
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

When too many tasks are in progress, everything slows down.

- **Example:** Multiple project setups, access fixes, and
  troubleshooting tasks all active at once — nothing finishes because
  everything competes for attention.

**Unplanned Work**
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Interrupts and urgent requests that bypass intake displace planned work.

- **Example:** Teams DMs, “quick asks,” urgent customer escalations, and
  leadership pings that pull staff away from project work.

**Unknown Dependencies**
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Work stops because you’re waiting on a system, team, or approval you
didn’t anticipate.

- **Example:** Storage provisioning delays, identity workflow approvals,
  Globus endpoint creation, or Slurm configuration steps that weren’t
  visible upfront.

**Conflicting Priorities**
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Competing requests push planned work aside without visibility.

- **Example:** Leadership asks override customer tickets;
  troubleshooting interrupts project setups; operational fixes compete
  with roadmap work.

**Neglected Work**
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Work that sits untouched because urgent items always win.

- **Example:** Documentation, onboarding improvements, cleanup tasks,
  audit prep — all repeatedly postponed because reactive work dominates.

This module helps teams surface those patterns in a lightweight,
practical way.

.. _section-7:

**Time Thief Identification Exercise**
--------------------------------------

This exercise is *inspired by* :mark:`book’s` emphasis on visibility,
flow, and pattern recognition but designed specifically for RCD work.

Run it for **3–5 days**.

**Step 1: Capture Disruptions in Real Time**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Each time your work is interrupted or delayed, jot down:

- What triggered the disruption

- Where it came from (Teams DM, leadership ask, customer ping, system
  issue)

- What work it displaced

- Whether the disruption was visible on your board

This helps surface **Unplanned Work** and **Conflicting Priorities** in
a way that reflects actual RCD workflows.

**Step 2: Mark Stalled Work on Your Board / Spreadsheet**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Add a simple “stalled” tag or color to any work item that:

- Hasn’t moved in 3+ days

- Is waiting on someone

- Is waiting on a system

- Keeps getting paused for other work

This reveals **Neglected Work** and **Too Much WIP** without needing a
formal WIP limit exercise.

**Step 3: Note Every Dependency That Appears**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Each time work stops because you’re waiting on something, capture:

- Who or what you’re waiting on

- Whether the dependency was known ahead of time

- Whether the dependency is visible on the board

This exposes **Unknown Dependencies** in a way that’s directly tied to
RCD systems (storage, identity, Globus, Slurm, API workflows).

**Step 4: Cluster What You Found Into Patterns**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

At the end of the observation window, group your notes into the five
Time Thieves:

- **Unplanned Work**

- **Unknown Dependencies**

- **Conflicting Priorities**

- **Neglected Work**

- **Too Much WIP**

This step is inspired by MWV’s emphasis on pattern recognition, but the
patterns you identify will be unique to your RCD environment.

**Step 5: Reflect on What Became Visible**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Use these questions to deepen insight:

- Which Time Thief showed up most often?

- Which ones displaced the most work?

- Which ones surprised you?

- Which ones were invisible before tracking?

- What small change could reduce one thief?

This is the “make it visible → learn → act” loop, adapted for RCD.

.. _start-small-2:

**Start Small**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Pick **one Time Thief** you suspect is affecting your team, such as
**Unplanned Work**, and track it for just a few days.

Let what becomes visible guide your next step.
