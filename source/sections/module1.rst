Module 1: Make Work Visible
===============================

Purpose
-----------

The first step toward improving workflow is making work visible.

Research Computing and Data (RCD) teams receive work from many sources,
including projects, support requests, operational responsibilities,
meetings, and strategic initiatives. When work is scattered across
emails, chats, tickets, documents, and individual memory, it becomes
difficult to understand workload, priorities, dependencies, and
capacity.

This module helps individuals and teams create a shared view of their
current and upcoming work. The goal is not to solve workflow problems
yet, but to create the visibility needed to have informed discussions
about priorities, dependencies, workload, and team health.

The goal of this module is to help individuals and teams create a
complete picture of their work so that informed discussions can occur
around priorities, capacity, dependencies, and team health.

Why This Matters
--------------------

Invisible work creates many of the challenges described in *Making Work
Visible*.

Without visibility:

- Teams take on too much work simultaneously.

- Unplanned work disrupts planned priorities.

- Dependencies remain hidden until they become blockers.

- Conflicting priorities create confusion and frustration.

- Important work becomes neglected and forgotten.

These conditions contribute to context switching, stress, delays, and
reduced productivity. The simple act of visualizing work often reveals
problems that previously went unnoticed. Teams discover work they forgot
about, identify tasks that have been stalled for weeks, and realize they
are carrying significantly more work than they can realistically
complete.

Making work visible does not solve every problem. It does, however, make
problems visible enough to discuss and address.

Learning Objectives
-----------------------

After completing this module, participants should be able to:

1. Identify and visualize current and upcoming work.

2. Recognize sources of unplanned work.

3. Surface dependencies that may delay progress.

4. Identify blocked and aging work items.

5. Assess individual and team workload.

6. Improve awareness of priorities and commitments.

7. Begin conversations around capacity, stress, and workflow
   improvement.

.. _section-1:

Recommended Approach
------------------------

This module uses a personal or team Kanban board as a visualization
tool.

Participants should list all active and upcoming work in a single
location. This can be done using physical sticky notes, a whiteboard,
spreadsheet, project management software, or the Project Kanban template
included in this toolkit.

Work items should be categorized into common workflow states:

**Not Started:**
Work that has been identified but has not yet begun.

**In Progress:**
Work that is actively receiving attention.

**Waiting / Review:**
Work that is awaiting feedback, approval, validation, or external input.

**Blocked:**
Work that cannot proceed because of a dependency, access issue, resource
limitation, or other obstacle.

**Completed:**
Work that has been finished and accepted.

Conducting the Exercise
---------------------------

Step 1: Capture Your Work
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

For one week, record:

- Current work items

- Upcoming work

- Project tasks

- Operational tasks

- Support requests

- Strategic initiatives

- Recurring responsibilities

Focus on capturing work that:

- Requires significant time or focus

- Involves collaboration

- Has dependencies

- Contributes to organizational goals

- May generate future work

The objective is not perfect accuracy. The objective is visibility.

Step 2: Evaluate Each Work Item
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

As work is collected, participants should consider the following
questions:

**Is the work item the right size?**

Work that is too small creates unnecessary clutter.

Work that is too large hides important details and dependencies.

For example:

**Too large:**

- Develop new storage service

**Better:**

- Gather requirements

- Create design document

- Implement provisioning workflow

- Complete testing plan

The goal is to identify work items that are meaningful, understandable,
and actionable.

**What dependencies exist?**

Dependencies may include:

- Other teams

- Vendors

- Researchers

- Leadership approvals

- Technical systems

- Infrastructure resources

Dependencies should be documented and made visible whenever possible.

**Is this unplanned work?**

Participants should identify work that arrived unexpectedly and document
its source.

Common examples include:

- Email requests

- Chat messages

- Leadership requests

- Emergencies and outages

- Customer escalations

This information helps reveal patterns in interruptions and reactive
work.

**Is there too much work in progress?**

One of the most common causes of context switching is having too many
active tasks at once.

Participants should review their board and ask:

   Am I working on more items than I can realistically complete?

**What work is blocked?**

Blocked work often reveals hidden dependencies and organizational
bottlenecks.

Understanding why work is blocked is often more valuable than simply
tracking completion.

**Are priorities clear?**

Every work item should have a understood priority.

When priorities are unclear, teams often default to the most recent
request rather than the most important work.

Reflection Questions
------------------------

After maintaining the board for approximately one week, consider the
following:

- What causes the most stress in my work?

- What surprised me when I visualized everything?

- How much WIP do I have?

- How much work arrived unexpectedly?

- Which tasks remained blocked the longest?

- Are my priorities clear?

- Where am I spending most of my time?

- What work is most at risk of becoming neglected?

Expected Outcomes
---------------------

By completing this module, teams should gain:

**Improved Visibility**

A shared understanding of current and upcoming work.

**Better Awareness of Capacity**

A clearer picture of team workload and work in progress.

**Increased Understanding of Dependencies**

Greater awareness of factors that delay or affect delivery.

**More Constructive Conversations**

Discussion shifts from assumptions and opinions toward visible evidence.

**Insights for Leadership**

Managers and organizational leaders gain a clearer understanding of team
accomplishments, workload, and capacity constraints.
