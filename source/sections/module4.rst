Module 4: Operationalize the Practice
-----------------------------------------

.. _purpose-7:

Purpose
~~~~~~~~~~~

The previous modules focused on teaching the principles of visual work
management. Participants learned how to identify work, break work into
manageable pieces, and recognize common workflow challenges.

This module introduces the Project-Level and Portfolio-Level Kanban
templates that can be used to operationalize those practices over time.

The goal is not to create another project management system. The goal is
to provide a lightweight framework that helps teams maintain visibility
into their work, identify workflow challenges, and support more informed
conversations about priorities, capacity, and dependencies.

The Project-Level Kanban is designed for teams and individuals who need
visibility into day-to-day project and operational work.

Each work item is tracked using common workflow states:

- Not Started

- In Progress

- Waiting / Review

- Blocked

- Completed

Additional fields help teams surface information that often remains
hidden, including priority, dependencies, blockers, unplanned work, and
aging.

**Suggested Uses**

- Project tracking

- Operational work

- Service improvements

- Technical initiatives

- Team planning discussions

The Portfolio-Level Kanban provides a higher-level view of work across a
group or organization.

Instead of tracking individual tasks, it tracks projects, initiatives,
and strategic efforts.

This view can help leaders:

- Understand overall workload

- Identify competing priorities

- Visualize portfolio balance

- Surface cross-team dependencies

- Recognize capacity constraints

The portfolio view is particularly useful when the number of active
efforts becomes difficult to understand through individual project
boards alone.

.. _expected-outcomes-2:

Expected Outcomes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

By implementing the Kanban templates, teams should be able to:

- Maintain visibility into current and upcoming work.

- Better understand workload and capacity.

- Surface dependencies and blockers earlier.

- Recognize patterns that affect productivity.

- Improve communication within the team and with leadership.

- Sustain the practices introduced throughout the toolkit.

Why Google Sheets?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The templates in this toolkit were intentionally built in Google Sheets
because most teams already have access to spreadsheets and can begin
using them immediately. The goal of this toolkit is not to introduce
another project management platform, but to help teams make work visible
using tools they already know.

These templates can be adapted to other platforms such as Excel, Jira,
Trello, Planner, Asana, or Smartsheet. The platform is less important
than the practices it supports: making work visible, surfacing
dependencies and blockers, tracking work in progress, and creating a
shared understanding of workload and priorities.

Sustaining the Practice
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Making work visible is relatively easy. Keeping work visible over time
is much harder.

Many workflow improvement efforts begin with enthusiasm but gradually
fall out of use as priorities shift and teams become busy. Common
barriers include:

- **Maintaining the board feels like extra work.**

  - **Solution:** Keep the process lightweight and focus on information
    that provides value to the team.

- **The information is not used in discussions or decisions.**

  - **Solution:** Review the board regularly and use it to guide
    conversations about priorities, blockers, dependencies, and
    workload.

- **Too much information is being tracked.**

  - **Solution:** Start with a small number of fields and add complexity
    only when it provides clear benefit.

- **Ownership is unclear.**

  - **Solution:** Establish shared responsibility for keeping work
    visible and current.

- **The practice never becomes a habit.**

  - **Solution:** Incorporate board reviews into existing team meetings
    and planning discussions.

.. _start-small-3:

Start Small
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Do not feel obligated to implement every element of this toolkit at
once. Begin with a few simple practices that provide immediate value,
then expand as the team becomes comfortable with the process.
