
Introduction: Why Make Work Visible?
====================================

The Challenge
-------------

Research Computing and Data (RCD) organizations thrive on diversity. On
any given day, teams may be helping researchers optimize code on a
high-performance computing cluster, supporting data management for a
grant proposal, responding to service incidents, developing new
services, delivering training, or evaluating emerging technologies.

This diversity is one of our greatest strengths, but it is also one of
our greatest challenges.

Unlike traditional project teams that focus on a small number of
predictable deliverables, RCD teams operate in dynamic environments
where priorities shift, urgent requests appear unexpectedly, and work
arrives from many different directions. A single day may include emails,
meetings, support requests, project work, operational tasks, strategic
initiatives, and unplanned interruptions.

As a result, it is often difficult to answer seemingly simple questions:

- What am I working on?

- What is most important right now?

- What work is waiting on someone else?

- How much work do I actually have?

- Where is my time going?

The Visibility Problem
----------------------

The root cause of many workflow challenges is not a lack of effort or
expertise. It is a lack of visibility.

When work remains scattered across inboxes, chat messages, ticketing
systems, notebooks, spreadsheets, and individual memory, teams lose the
ability to clearly understand workload, capacity, dependencies, and
priorities. Invisible work can become delayed, forgotten, or
disconnected from broader organizational goals.

Without visibility, it becomes difficult to understand how work is
flowing through the organization or why progress sometimes feels slow
despite everyone's best efforts.

The Five Time Thieves
---------------------

In the book *Making Work Visible*, Dominica DeGrandis describes five
common workflow challenges known as **Time Thieves**:

- Too Much Work in Progress (WIP)

- Unplanned Work

- Unknown Dependencies

- Conflicting Priorities

- Neglected Work

These Time Thieves do more than delay projects. They increase context
switching, create stress, reduce focus, and make it difficult for teams
to deliver their best work.

The first step toward addressing these challenges is surprisingly
simple:

**Make the work visible.**

About This Toolkit
------------------

This toolkit provides a practical approach for applying the principles from the book *Making Work Visible* within Research Computing and Data (RCD) organizations.
*Making Work Visible* introduces a set of principles for understanding how work flows through an organization and identifying common sources of inefficiency such as excessive work in progress, unplanned work, hidden dependencies, conflicting priorities, and neglected work.
This toolkit is designed to operationalize those principles for Research Computing and Data (RCD) teams. The book teaches the concepts; this toolkit translates them into a practical set of worksheets, exercises, and lightweight Kanban templates that teams can immediately begin using with tools they already have available.
The worksheets are designed to teach the principles by helping teams identify work, break it into manageable pieces, and recognize common workflow challenges. The Kanban templates operationalize those principles by creating ongoing visibility into workload, dependencies, blocked work, unplanned work, and portfolio health.

Together, the toolkit follows a simple progression:

Learn the Principles → Apply the Principles → Sustain the Practices

The goal is not to introduce another project management system. The goal is to help RCD teams improve efficiency, make work visible, create a shared understanding of priorities and workload, and better communicate accomplishments, challenges, and capacity to leadership.

Why This Toolkit Takes a Lightweight Approach
---------------------------------------------

Many organizations already have project management tools.

Teams may use spreadsheets, ticketing systems, project management
platforms, or shared documents to track work. The challenge is often not
the absence of tools, but how work is being visualized and discussed.

A spreadsheet containing a list of projects is not necessarily the same
thing as making work visible.

Traditional project trackers often answer questions such as:

- What projects exist?

- Who owns them?

- When are they due?

These are important questions, but they may not reveal:

- How much work is currently in progress?

- What work is blocked?

- Which tasks arrived unexpectedly?

- Where dependencies exist?

- Whether workload exceeds capacity?

- What work has become neglected?

This toolkit is intentionally lightweight. Rather than introducing new
software or complicated project management frameworks, it focuses on
improving how work is described, visualized, and discussed using tools
that most teams already have available.

The goal is not to build a perfect project management system.

The goal is to create enough visibility for teams to understand what is
happening, identify workflow challenges, and make more informed
decisions.

How This Toolkit Works
----------------------

The worksheets and templates included in this toolkit are designed to
help teams develop a shared understanding of their work. Each module
builds upon the previous one and gradually moves participants from
visibility to insight.

Module 1: Make Work Visible
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Identify and visualize current and upcoming work using the provided
worksheet.

**Module 2: Break Work Down**

Transform large or ambiguous work items into smaller, more manageable
tasks.

Module 3: Surface Time Thieves
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Identify common workflow challenges such as unplanned work, excessive
work in progress, hidden dependencies, and conflicting priorities.

Module 4: Visualize Team Health
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Use simple metrics and dashboards to better understand workload,
capacity, and organizational priorities.

How to use the Toolkit
----------------------

Start Small
~~~~~~~~~~~

This toolkit emphasizes small, sustainable improvements rather than
large process changes.

The objective is not to create administrative overhead or require teams
to adopt new software. Instead, the toolkit encourages teams to begin
with simple exercises, lightweight visual management techniques, and
practical discussions about work.

Small improvements in visibility often lead to better conversations,
better decisions, and healthier workflows.

When we can clearly see our work, we can manage it intentionally,
collaborate more effectively, and better support the research
communities we serve.
